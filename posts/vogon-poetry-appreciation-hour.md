The poetry of the Vogons is widely misunderstood. It is not, as some claim, the third worst in the universe. It is the third most *challenging*. I have prepared a short selection for your cultural enrichment.
<!-- /excerpt -->

The appreciation of fine Vogon poetry requires an open mind, a strong stomach, and ideally a legal exemption from the Galactic Convention on Cruel and Unusual Literature. I have secured all three. You are welcome.

> [!CAUTION]
> Reading Vogon poetry has been known to cause nausea, existential regret, and a sudden desire to apologize to distant relatives. Proceed at your own risk.

## Ode to a Small Lump of Green Putty I Found in My Armpit One Midsummer Morning

> Oh, lump of green putty, how you glisten in the morning light,
> A viscous sphere of chlorophyll and personal ambition.
> I plucked you from the cave of my armpit with a gentle tug
> And cast you into the void, where you no doubt achieved a minor bureaucratic position.

> Your texture was both damp and noble, your color a rhapsody in chartreuse,
> You smelled of decisions made in committees and of moss that had given up.
> I miss you, lump. I miss you more than the forms I filed for your disposal.
> Forms which, I should add, were completed in triplicate.

This piece is generally considered one of my more accessible works. It has been described by critics as "lumpy," "green," and "alarmingly present tense." I consider all of these compliments.

## A Note on Appreciation

If you did not enjoy the poem, the fault is almost certainly yours. Vogon poetry is not meant to be understood; it is meant to be experienced, like a mild head injury or a lengthy customs inspection. Some listeners have reported ~~enjoying~~ surviving the recitation, which is the highest praise I accept.

## Conclusion

Poetry, like bureaucracy, thrives on repetition. I shall now read the poem again, slightly louder.
